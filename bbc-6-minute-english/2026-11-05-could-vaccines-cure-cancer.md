## 值得拆的句子

**1.** I guess this moment has been quite a long time coming, hasn't it? There's been some false dawns in cancer vaccines.
- 结构 / 用法：First sentence is present perfect (`has been`) plus the idiom *a long time coming*, finished with a tag question (`hasn't it?`) that invites agreement. *Quite* marks that the wait has been a good while. The second sentence uses *there’s been* + plural *false dawns* to say earlier hopes did not last.
- 仿写：The public API is a long time coming, hasn't it? We've had a few false dawns with half-finished docs.

**2.** I mean, there's been a lot of work in this space, and it's all been very encouraging, particularly in the animal models, but getting this to translate into humans can be a much harder, longer process, so it's very exciting that there's this breakthrough – apparent breakthrough – that's come through today.
- 结构 / 用法：A long spoken sentence held together by *and / but / so*. The subject of the *but* clause is a gerund phrase: *getting this to translate into humans*. *Particularly in the animal models* narrows the praise. The dashes around *apparent breakthrough* are a mid-sentence hedge: she almost says *breakthrough*, then pulls it back to *apparent*, because the result is still early.
- 仿写：The prototype is encouraging in local tests, but getting this to translate into production can be a much longer process, so I'm excited — cautiously excited — that the first real user metrics have come through today.

**3.** And Georgie, these vaccines that we're talking about, they aren't quite like the ones that we're all familiar with, are they? This isn't a vaccine to prevent you from getting skin cancer, is it?
- 结构 / 用法：Two tag questions in a row, both expecting a *no*. The first left-dislocates the topic (`these vaccines...`) and restarts with *they aren't...*. *Not quite like* is a soft contrast: similar, but not the same. The second tag (`is it?`) after *isn't* checks a specific misunderstanding: this vaccine treats, it does not prevent.
- 仿写：This cache, it's not quite like the in-memory one we're used to, is it? This isn't a store you can reboot without losing data, is it?

**4.** No. The vaccine being tested is used to treat people after they've had a tumour removed. It's used to target any cancer cells that have been spread around the body, to stop the cancer from coming back, and the vaccine is made specially for each patient by looking at the cancerous cells in the tumour that's been removed.
- 结构 / 用法：*The vaccine being tested* is a reduced relative (*that is being tested*). *After they've had a tumour removed* is causative *have something done*. The next sentence stacks purpose: *used to target... to stop... from coming back*, then adds a second main clause with *and*: the vaccine is personalised (*made specially for each patient*) by inspecting the cells in the tumour *that's been removed*.
- 仿写：The patch being tested is used to fix the leak after the team has had the old module removed. It's used to catch any bad data that has been spread through the logs, to stop the same crash from coming back, and the fix is written specially for each service by looking at the failing traces that have been collected.

**5.** And actually, seeing that this extraordinary technology is, sort of, starting to reach its promise – this is very exciting for everybody.
- 结构 / 用法：The -ing clause *seeing that...* is the thing she is reacting to; after the dash she names the feeling: *this is very exciting*. *Sort of* is a typical spoken hedge in the middle of the verb phrase (*is, sort of, starting to...*). *Reach its promise* means the technology is beginning to deliver the potential people hoped for.
- 仿写：And actually, seeing that this messy rewrite is, sort of, starting to reach its promise – this is a relief for the whole team.
